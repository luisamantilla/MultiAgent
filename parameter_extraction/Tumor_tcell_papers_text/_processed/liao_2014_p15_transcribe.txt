If $I_t$ is constant, then the solution is $d(t)=e^{\lambda_1 t - \eta_t \frac{I_t + s_c}{I_t}} d(0)$

which leads to

$$\eta_t = [\lambda_1 - \eta_t] - \frac{1}{c(0)} \int_0^t c(t) \frac{I_t + s_c}{I_t} \, dt. \tag{26}$$

Since $c(5)/c(0)$ is close to 1 and the range of $I_t$ may vary from 100pg/cm$^3$ to 3400pg/cm$^3$ [30]:558-T1 tumor cells and IL-10$^{-/-}$ P1CTL T cells or from 750pg/cm$^3$ to 1600pg/cm$^3$ for 3.5B1-IL27 tumor cells and IL-10$^{-/-}$/P1CTL/IL27, we choose in Equation (26)

$$\frac{I_t + s_c}{I_t} \approx 2.$$

Recalling that $\lambda_1 - \mu_c = 0.295/day$, we take $\eta_t = 0.6/day$.

Next, we choose $s_c = 150pg/cm^3$ and proceed to estimate $\eta_t$ by considering T cells P1CTL and P1CTL/IL-27 which generate $I_t$. For (25), if $I_{10}$ and $\frac{I_t}{I_t + s_c}$ are constants, then the solution is

$$d(t) = e^{\lambda_1 t - \eta_t \frac{I_t + s_c}{I_t}} d(0),$$

and hence

$$\eta_t = [\lambda_1 - \mu_c - \eta_t] - \frac{1}{c(0)} \int_0^t c(t) \frac{I_{10} + s_c}{I_{10}} \, dt/day$$

$$\approx (0.295/day - 0.6/day) \times \frac{I_t}{I_t + s_c} \times \frac{I_{10} + s_c}{I_{10}}.$$

The concentration of $I_t$ with $I_{10}$ is smaller than the concentration of $I_t$ where $I_{10}$ is blocked [30]. We take $\frac{I_t}{I_t + s_c} \approx \frac{1}{3}$.

In [30], the concentration of $I_{10}$ vary from 1pg/cm$^3$ to 2000pg/cm$^3$. We take $s_c = 150pg/cm^3$, so that $\frac{I_{10} + s_c}{I_{10}} \approx 3.5$; hence $\eta_t = 0.345/day$.
