cells were washed twice in flow buffer and fixed in 0.6 ml of
2% paraformaldehyde. Each sample was assayed for 4 min
by flow cytometry (Epics XL, Coulter, Fullerton, CA) and
the data were analyzed by WinMDI® (Joe Trotter, Scripps
Institute, La Jolla, CA).

The data were analyzed by first comparing the forward
scatter versus side scatter density plots for adjuvant only
and for media-treated DCs. These plots give an indication
of where the adjuvant population and DC population will be
located in the samples that contain both adjuvant particles
and DCs. In general, the DCs have a higher intensity forward
scatter than adjuvants. A density plot of forward scatter ver-
sus side scatter of a sample of DCs treated with BODIPY
FL labeled AC adsorbed to adjuvant was then constructed
and a gate was made around the DC population. Next a plot
of red fluorescence versus green fluorescence for the gated
DC population was constructed. There were two populations
observed; one at high red fluorescence and one at low red
fluorescence. The high red fluorescence population were ac-
tivated DCs who express high levels of MHCII. The low red
fluorescence population were immature DCs which express
low levels of MHC II [9]. Immature DCs are the cell popu-
lation of interest in this experiment and the low fluorescence
cells were gated for the gated population. This procedure was
repeated for each time point of each sample.

2.9. Degree of adsorption of AC in cell culture media

The degree of adsorption of AC by each adjuvant follow-
ing dilution with cell culture media was studied by adding
0.6 ml of each model vaccine (from Section 2.5) to 2.4 ml
of cell culture media in a six well plate and mixing by gen-
tle agitation to uniformly distribute the adjuvant. Samples
were incubated at 37 °C without mixing. After 3 h, super-
natants (100 μl) were obtained by centrifuging at 10,000 rpm
(16,750 × g) for 15 min. The concentration of AC in the su-
pernatants was determined by ELISA. Sheep anti-bovine
casein (Biodesign International, Saco, ME) was used as
the coating antibody, rabbit anti-bovine casein (Immunol-
ogy Consultants Laboratory, Newburg, OR) was used as
the capture antibody and goat anti-rabbit IgG conjugated to
horseradish peroxidase (Sigma, St. Louis, MO) was used as
the detection antibody.

2.10. Aggregate size

The aggregate size of each adjuvant in cell culture me-
dia was determined by adding 0.6 ml of each model vaccine
(from Section 2.5) to 2.4 ml of cell culture media in a six
well plate and mixing by gentle agitation to uniformly dis-
tribute the adjuvant. Samples were incubated at 37 °C for
3 h and examined by fluorescent microscopy (0 100, Nikon,
Tokyo). The mean diameter of the adjuvant aggregates was
determined by image analysis using Image-Pro Plus Version
5.0 (MediaCybernetics, Silver Spring, MD).

3. Results and discussion

3.1. Surface charge of adjuvants

Evidence for the adsorption of phosphate by AH in PTAH-
1 and PTAH-2 is seen in Table 1 as the zeta potential of AH,
which is normally positive [17], became increasingly nega-
tive as the concentration of phosphate used to pretreat AH in-
creased. The negative surface charge of PTAH-2 approached
that of AP [17].

3.2. Adsorption isotherms

Adsorption isotherms of AC by the four adjuvants were
determined because AC has an isoelectric point of 4.6 [18] and is nega-
tively charged at pH 7.4. Therefore, an electrostatic attrac-
tive force will exist between AC and AH while an electro-
static repulsive force will operate between AC and PTAH-1,
PTAH-2 and AP. As seen in Table 1, AC was adsorbed by
each adjuvant although the adsorptive capacity decreased as
the concentration of phosphate used in the pretreatment in-
creased. The adsorption of negatively charged AC by neg-
atively charged PTAH-1, PTAH-2 and AP occurred in spite
of electrostatic repulsive forces. Iyer et al. [19] recently re-
ported that AC is adsorbed by AH and phosphate-treated AH
by ligand exchange of phosphate groups of AC with surface
hydroxyls of the adjuvant. AC adsorption by the four adju-
vants in this study was probably also by ligand exchange.
