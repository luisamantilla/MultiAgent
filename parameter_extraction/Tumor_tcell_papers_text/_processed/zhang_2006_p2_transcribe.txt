Results  

Impaired Survival of Spic6-Deficient CTLs  
To address the role of the GrB inhibitor Spic6 (Serpin b9)  
(Sun et al., 1997) in the protection of CTLs from self-  
inflicted damage, we generated Spic6-deficient mice.  
By means of homologous recombination in ES cells  
from C57BL/6 mice (B6 mice), deleted exon 7, which  
encodes 60% of Spic6 and includes the critical reactive  
center loop (RCL), which is required for target protease  

inhibition (Sun et al., 1997) (Figures 1A and 1B). By Cre-  
mediated recombination, the G418-resistance cassette  
was removed (Figure 1C) to avoid affecting the tran-  
scription of closely linked serpin genes such as Spic3  
(Serpin b6a) (Sun et al., 1997; Kaiserman et al., 2002).  
Mice homozygous for Spic6 mutant alleles in the B6  
background (Spic6 KO mice) were generated (Figure 1D).  
Real-time PCR for the Spic6 RCL region revealed the ex-  
pression of Spic6 mRNA in both myeloid and lymphoid  
cells from B6 but not Spic6 KO mice (Figure 1E). The mu-  
tation of the Spic6 locus did not affect the expression of  
Spic3 mRNA in Spic6 KO splenocytes (Figure 1F). The de-  
velopment of leukocytes was not affected by the Spic6  
mutation because Spic6 KO mice did not display any sig-  
nificant difference in the number of myeloid or lymphoid  
cells in the thymus or blood (see Tables S1 and S2 in the  
Supplemental Data available with this article online).  
Spic6 is upregulated in CTLs, and overexpression can  
inhibit GrB-mediated apoptosis in vitro (Phillips et al.,  
2004). To examine the physiological role of Spic6, we ex-  
amined the CTL response of Spic6 KO mice to infection.
