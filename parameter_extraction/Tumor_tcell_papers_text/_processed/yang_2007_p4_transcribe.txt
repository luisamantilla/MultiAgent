expected, CCL5-induced iDC migration was also inhibited
by PTX.

The migration of muscle cells in response to HMGB1 is
mediated by RAGE [20, 21]. To test if HMGB1-induced iDC
migration was also dependent on RAGE, we examined if anti-
RAGE antibody could suppress iDC chemotaxis in response to
HMGB1 (Fig. 4B). Although isotype-matched control mouse
IgG2b did not, anti-RAGE antibody dose-dependently reduced
rhHMGB1-induced migration of iDCs with significant inhibition
at 5 and 20 µg/ml, suggesting the participation of RAGE in
mediating the chemotactic effect of HMGB1 on DCs. As
HMGB1 induced the migration of iDCs but not mDCs (Fig. 2),
we further investigated whether the failure of mDCs to migrate
to HMGB1 might be a result of a down-regulation of RAGE
expression on the surface of mDCs by flow cytometry. As
expected, iDCs were stained negative for CD83 and positive for
RAGE (Fig. 4C, upper panels). DCs treated with LPS not only
became mature (CD83+) but also significantly reduced their
surface expression of RAGE (Fig. 4C, lower panels). Therefore,
HMGB1-induced DC chemotaxis seemed to be dependent on
Gi protein and RAGE.
