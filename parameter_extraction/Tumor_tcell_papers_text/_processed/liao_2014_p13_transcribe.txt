c(0) = 5 \times 10^8 cell/cm^2. \tag{12}

There is no data in [30] on the density of the tumor in day 5. We assume that this density is larger than c(0) but substantively smaller than the maximal capacity 10^9 cell/cm^2. We take

c(5) = 5.05 \times 10^8 cell/cm^2, \tag{13}

for J558-Ctrl with PICTL or J558-IL-27 with PICTL/IL-27, but

c(5) = 5.1 \times 10^8 cell/cm^2, \tag{14}

for J558-Ctrl with IL-10^-/PICTL or J558-IL-27 with IL-10^-/PICTL/IL-27, since the last two types of T cells do not generate I_{27}.

From Figure 1a in [30], there were 1.1 \times 10^6 PICTL at day 1 and 2.6 \times 10^6 PICTL at day 5; 10^6 PICTL/IL-27 at day 1 and 5 \times 10^6 PICTL/IL-27 at day 5. We assume that these CD8^+ T cells occupy the volume of 10 mm^2 for the first 5 days. Hence

T(1) = 1.1 \times 10^8 cell/cm^2, T(5) = 2.6 \times 10^8 cell/cm^2, \tag{15}

for PICTL

T(1) = 10^8 cell/cm^2, T(5) = 5 \times 10^8 cell/cm^2, \tag{16}

for PICTL/IL-27.

Estimate of the parameters in (1)

Since IL-27 belongs to the IL-12 family, we take its diffusion coefficient and the degradation rate to be the same as for IL-12 [48]:

D_{27} = 1.25 \times 10^{-3} cm^2/day,

\mu_{27} = 2/day.

In order to find a_{27}, we use the simplified version of Equation (1):

\frac{dI_{27}(t)}{dt} = a_{27}c - \mu_{27}I_{27}(t).

If c is taken to be a constant, then

I_{27}(5) = e^{-5\mu_{27}}I_{27}(0) + \frac{a_{27}c}{\mu_{27}}(1 - e^{-5\mu_{27}}). \tag{17}

From Figure 1A in [30], I_{27}(0) = 5 \times 10^8 pg/cm^3 and only 5 - 10\% of I_{27} remained in day 5. We assume that 7.5\% of I_{27} remained at day 5, so that

I_{27}(0) = 5 \times 10^8 pg/cm^3 and I_{27}(5) = 3750 pg/cm^3. \tag{18}

Taking c to be the average between the values at day 0 and 5 (see (12) and (13)) and recalling (17), we get

3750 pg/cm^3 = e^{-5\mu_{27} \times 2/day} \times 5 \times 10^8 pg/cm^3

+ \frac{a_{27}}{2/day} \times (5.025 \times 10^8) cell/cm^3 \times (1 - e^{-5\mu_{27} \times 2/day}),

so that a_{27} = 1.5 \times 10^{-5} pg/cell/day.

Estimate of the parameters in (2)

We consider a simplified version of Equation (2):

\frac{dI_{10}(t)}{dt} = s_{10}T + a_{10}T \frac{I_{27}}{T + q_{10}} - \mu_{10}I_{10}(t). \tag{19}

From [48], we have \mu_{10} = 16/day. To estimate s_{10}, we consider the case of J558-Ctrl tumor cells, for which the term with I_{27} is removed from (19):

\frac{dI_{10}(t)}{dt} = s_{10}T - \mu_{10}I_{10}(t).

If T is constant, then

I_{10}(5) = e^{-4\mu_{10}}I_{10}(1) + \frac{s_{10}T}{\mu_{10}}(1 - e^{-4\mu_{10}}).

From the profile of PICTL in Figure 3D of [30], we have I_{10}(1) = 300 pg/cm^3 at day 1 and I_{10}(5) = 1 pg/cm^3 at day 5 and we take T to be the mean value of T(1) and T(5) in (15). We then get

1 pg/cm^3 = e^{-4\mu_{10} \times 16/day} \times 300 pg/cm^3 + \frac{s_{10} \times 1.8 \times 10^8 cell/cm^3}{16/day}

\times (1 - e^{-4\mu_{10} \times 16/day}),

so that s_{10} = 8.89 \times 10^{-8} pg/cell/day.

Next, we choose q_{10} = 5 \times 10^8 pg/cm^3 and proceed to compute a_{10}. We then consider J558-IL-27 tumor cells which can generate I_{27}. For simplicity, we take T to be the average between the values at days 1 and 5 (see (16)) and I_{27} to be the average between the values at days 0 and 5 (see (18):

T = 3 \times 10^8 cell/cm^3 and I_{27} = 26875 pg/cm^3.
