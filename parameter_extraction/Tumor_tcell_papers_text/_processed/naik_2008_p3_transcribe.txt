Figure 1 Location and developmental pathways of the dendritic cell (DC) subtypes. (a) The location of precursors of DC (pre-DC) and DC subtypes in a steady-state mouse. (b) Hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) and their progenitors—including common myeloid progenitor (CMP) and common lymphoid progenitor (CLP)—give rise to more committed progenitors, including the macrophage-DC precursor (MDP) and pro-DC. MDPs give rise to both monocyte subtypes, which can differentiate into DCs. This latter process occurs at a low level in the steady state, and is enhanced with inflammation. Development of these DCs can be recapitulated in bone marrow (BM) cultures with granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) (± IL-4, ± TGF-β). Pro-DCs (CD11c+ MHCIIlo) divide and differentiate into pre-DCs (CD11c+ MHCIIint) en route to generation of DC subtypes, and this can be mimicked in mice in vivo. Stage 3 (fl3) ligand BM cultures. As both of these cell types are found in BM and lymphoid organs, it is not clear where this differentiation step takes place. In any case, pre-conventional DCs (pre-cDCs) in spleen have committed precursors to either CD8+ cDC only (CD24hi pre-cDC) or CD8- cDC only (CD24lo pre-cDCs). Some, but not all, DCs are capable of low-level homeostatic self-renewal in the steady state. Most splenic DC are replaced by blood precursors within 10–14 days, whereas dermal and Langerhans cells (LCs) are stable and not replaced. The situations changes in cases of inflammation, infection or DC loss. In these circumstances, monocytes can be precursors for LCs and inflammatory tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)-producing ‘Tip’ DCs.

In two landmark papers,40,41 separate Ly6Chi and Ly6Clo monocytes were described that correspond to human CD14hi and CD16+CD14+ monocytes, respectively. In both mice and humans, monocytes have the capacity to differentiate into macrophages in response to M-CSF,47 and into CD11c+ MHCII+ DCs in the presence of GM-CSF, with or without interleukin (IL)-4.47,48,55 This DC transition can occur for both monocyte subsets in vitro, and under some conditions in vivo.40,41,56–58

As such, monocytes, or their earlier myeloid precursors, were thought of as the definitive pre-DCs, and the DCs derived in vitro with GM-CSF as representative of those found normally in a mouse or patient. However, transfer of Ly6Chi monocytes into steady-state or irradiated mice were never able to give appreciable DC generation,40,41,56,59 except for two studies where low-level generation was observed.57,60 Transfer of the resident Ly6Clo subtype could produce some DCs in spleen, lung and lamina propria.40,60,61 However, in some of these cases, inflammation of the experimental clearance of DCs was required for efficient engraftment. Whether these latter situations represent an ‘emergency’ process, or simply amplify the normal process, is not clear. The precise role of monocytes in DC generation in vitro and in vivo is discussed later.
