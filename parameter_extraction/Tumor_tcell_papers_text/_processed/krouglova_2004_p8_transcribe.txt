FIGURE 7 Interaction of Flutax2 and tubulin in MES buffer determined by FCS. Flutax2 at nanomolar concentration (13 nM) is added to different concentrations of tubulin (between 250 nM and 50 μM) in MES buffer. The ratio of bound/free Flutax2 is increasing with increasing tubulin concentration. The association constant can be obtained from the slope of the curve.

In our case, the experiments are done at low Mg\(^{2+}\) concentration and the added tubulin is always dimeric. The amount of the tubulin consumed in the oligomers induced by paclitaxel can be neglected compared to the total tubulin concentration. Therefore the free tubulin concentration can be assumed to be the total concentration and we can define the following equation

\[
K_{ass}[Tu] = \frac{[Tu.FP]}{[FP]},
\]

(10)

where \([FP]\) is the concentration of free fluorescent paclitaxel, \([Tu]\) is the concentration of free tubulin and \([Tu.FP]\) is the concentration of fluorescent paclitaxel present in complex. The ratio of concentrations is equal to the ratio of amplitudes, when there is no fluorescence change upon binding.

However, when the fluorescent paclitaxel was added to tubulin, a reduction of the fluorescence signal per molecule was observed. This means that the fraction of paclitaxel in complex with tubulin, estimated from the amplitude fractions of the autocorrelation function, is underestimated, and the following equation for the calculating of the association constant has to be used (Van Craenenbroeck and Engelborghs, 1999):

\[
K_{ass}[Tu] = \left( \frac{y}{1-y} \right) \frac{1}{\alpha^2},
\]

(11)

with

\[
\alpha = \left( \frac{\langle fpm \rangle}{fpm_{0} - \text{Flutax2}} \right) \left( \frac{\langle fpm \rangle}{fpm_{0} - \text{Free-Flutax2}} \right),
\]

(12)

where \(y\) and \(1-y\) are amplitude fractions of bound and free fluorescent paclitaxel as measured by FCS, respectively, and \(\alpha\) is the correction factor that can be determined when the fluorescence signal per molecule (\(fpm\)) for free and bound ligand is known.

In our case, the concentration of tubulin could not be increased enough to saturate fluorescent paclitaxel completely and thus the signal per molecule of bound fluorescent paclitaxel could not be determined directly. Therefore, an extrapolation of the fluorescence per particle to high tubulin concentrations was carried out, by fitting the fluorescence per particle to a saturation function (Van Craenenbroeck and Engelborghs, 1999):

\[
fpm = \frac{fpm_{0} + fpm_{\infty} K_{ass}[Tu]}{1 + K_{ass}[Tu]}
\]

(13)

where \(fpm\) is the average fluorescence signal per molecule and \(fpm_{0}\) and \(fpm_{\infty}\) are the fluorescence per molecule of free and bound Flutax2, respectively. The parameter \(fpm_{0}\) allowed the calculation of an \(\alpha\) value of 0.41. (The same decrease in specific fluorescence was observed when Flutax2 was quantitatively bound to microtubules; see below). When this correction was considered in Eq. 11, \(K_{ass}\) was determined to be (0.12 ± 0.01) μM\(^{-1}\). The association constant obtained from the fitting of the fluorescence per molecule (Eq. 13), was determined to be 0.14 μM\(^{-1}\), which is in good agreement with the corrected association constant obtained from the fraction measurements (Eq. 11).

It should be noted that a small deviation from linearity occurs near the beginning of the binding plot (Fig. 7). We believe that this behavior of the plot can be explained by the heterogeneity of the system: added tubulin initially only binds to paclitaxel, and later also to the already formed tubulin-paclitaxel complex. Another possibility that cannot be excluded is that the fluorescence decrease differs for the first and the subsequent tubulin molecules binding to Flutax2.

The interactions of Flutax2 with tubulin are also examined under nonassembly conditions: in MES buffer in the absence of Mg\(^{2+}\) ions and in the presence of podophyllotoxin (Schilstra et al., 1989). Podophyllotoxin is known as a drug that interacts with tubulin and prevents microtubule formation (Schilstra et al., 1989; Sackett, 1993). But in our measurements we found that the tubulin-podophyllotoxin complex can also form oligomers in the presence of nanomolar concentration of Flutax2. The diffusion coefficient of the tubulin-fluorescent paclitaxel complex is the same as for tubulin previously saturated with podophyllotoxin and then measured with fluorescent derivatives of paclitaxel by FCS (Fig. 4). No significant difference is found between tubulin and tubulin incubated with podophyllotoxin in the binding of Flutax2.
